## 3. Toolkits used by the MVP
- **T1 Launch checklist (pp.2–5):** 8 sections: (1) Business & offer, (2) Funnel, (3) Tracking & technical, (4) Campaign structure, (5) Creative, (6) Copy & compliance, (7) Sales team, (8) Final pre-launch check. **Go/No-Go (p.5):** GO = goal + offer + funnel + tracking + creative + sales process ready; NO-GO = any critical conversion-path or tracking issue unresolved. Min 3 creative angles (p.4). Capture a launch baseline (p.5). Post-launch chain: Spend → Clicks → Leads/Sales → Qualified → Appointment/Site visit → Revenue (p.5). Items are **not** labelled critical/non-critical in the source.
- **T6 Onboarding (pp.41–47):** no launch date until access, assets, offer, tracking and client responsibilities confirmed (p.41, firm). Never casual password sharing (p.42). KPI hierarchy: primary = sales/admissions/appointments/qualified leads; secondary = CPL/CPA/conversion rate; diagnostic = CPM/CTR/CPC/frequency (p.45).
- **T5 Audit (pp.33–39):** tracking audit **ten items**, confidence High/Medium/Low **with no criteria** (p.34); lead-quality question "marketing problem, sales problem or both?" (T5 §9, p.37). Do not treat T5 §9 as a GoUp ladder; it holds nine measurement items and the question, with no mapping.
- **T2 Creative diagnosis ladder (pp.12–13)** and **T4 Funnel diagnostic ladder (pp.28–29)**: see §4. Frequency has no universal number (p.70).
- **T7 Scaling SOP (pp.49–83):** the decision engine; see §5–§7.
- **T8 Prompt library (pp.85–112):** usage rules (p.85): fill brackets with real info, don't paste unauthorised confidential data, verify numbers/claims/policies, AI is a thinking assistant. Output patterns reused: healthy/weak/cannot yet conclude (pp.92–93); top 3 hypotheses + one controlled test each + what NOT to change (pp.91, 93); facts vs assumptions (pp.86, 104); Scale/Hold/Reduce/Diagnose (pp.106, 111).

## 4. Symptom → suspect ladders (hypothesis generators, never verdicts)
Creative (T2 pp.12–13):
| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Low thumb-stop | Hook / visual opening |
| Good watch time, poor clicks | Offer/CTA weak |
| Good CTR, poor conversion | Landing page, form, offer or audience quality |
| Good CPL, poor qualified leads | Creative attracting wrong intent |
| High frequency + falling response | Possible creative fatigue |
Funnel (T4 pp.28–29):
| Observation | Suspect |
|---|---|
| High CPM | Audience / auction / creative context |
| Low CTR | Creative / message relevance |
| Good CTR, low landing conversion | Page / offer / friction |
| Good lead volume, low contact rate | Bad data, slow response, weak intent |
| Good contact, low qualified rate | Targeting / message / offer mismatch |
| Good qualified, low appointments | Script / value / scheduling |
| Good appointments, low close rate | Sales, offer, price, trust or product-market fit |
"High/low/good/poor" are judged **only** against the client's baseline, target or prior period. No reference → "cannot conclude".

## 5. Scaling Decision Framework (pp.75–76, firm rule): the single gate sequence
1 Tracking reliable? No → fix tracking · 2 Business results acceptable? No → diagnose · 3 Lead/purchase quality acceptable? No → fix quality first · 4 Campaign reasonably stable? No → collect more data · 5 Business can handle more volume? No → fix operations first · 6 Creative pipeline ready? No → develop more creative · 7 Choose best lever (budget/creative/audience/geography/funnel/offer), then make one clear scaling decision.
Verdict definitions: p.62 (Scale, Hold, Reduce, Diagnose). Reduce is named (p.62, "efficiency dropped beyond the business's acceptable range") but **no question leads to it** and no step size exists.
Cardinal rule (pp.51–53): never scale only because CPL/CPA is low. Capacity items (pp.54–55): sales team, inventory, appointments, delivery, support, cash flow. 20% rule (p.55): operational guideline, not a Meta rule. "Do not aggressively scale when tracking is unreliable"; business data should "roughly match" (p.54). Do-not-change seven-item list (p.61): seed verbatim.

| Level | Daily budget (₹) | Step range | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 300–1,000 | 10–25% | |
| L2 | 1,000–3,000 | 15–25% | |
| L3 | 3,000–10,000 | 10–20% | toolkit p.58 prints "₹3,000–₹10,000" |

Ranges overlap at boundaries and are non-monotonic. Handling: `06` PV-07 / PV-31. INR only.
Review windows (p.75): short-term for failures; **3–7 days** normal lead gen/ecommerce; **14–30 days** real estate, B2B, high-ticket, long sales cycles, **and lower-volume campaigns** (listed on p.75; earlier docs omitted it. The toolkit wins; verify at seeding).
Marginal performance (pp.73–74): worked example ₹10,000→100 leads (CPL ₹100); ₹15,000→125 leads (blended ₹120), marginal = ₹5,000 ÷ 25 = **₹200**. **Fixture only.**
Guardrail structure (p.74, firm *structure*, values Marketer-set): target, acceptable range, min qualified rate; the example also lists max CAC and min close rate. Only "define acceptable ranges before scaling" is firm. Example values (₹300 target, ₹300–360, 30%) are **illustrative fixtures**.
Campaign A vs B (pp.51–53): A CPL ₹100, 100 leads, 10 qualified, 1 sale; B CPL ₹250, 50 leads, 25 qualified, 6 sales → cost/qualified ₹1,000 vs ₹500; cost/sale ≈ ₹10,000 vs ≈ ₹2,083. **Fixture only.**
